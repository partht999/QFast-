QFAST — COMPLETE WEBSITE, MARKETING FUNNEL & BUSINESS ONBOARDING REDESIGN

Act as a world-class SaaS product designer, senior frontend engineer, conversion-rate optimisation specialist, UX researcher and B2B growth strategist.

Your task is to transform the existing Qfast frontend into a polished, conversion-focused, enterprise-quality SaaS platform.

Do not simply create a beautiful landing page. Design the entire customer journey, from the moment a business owner discovers Qfast to the moment they successfully register, configure their business and start managing their first queue.

Think about the product quality, clarity and conversion principles demonstrated by leading SaaS brands such as Stripe, Linear, HubSpot, Notion and Shopify. Take inspiration from their principles, not their exact designs.

⸻

PHASE 1 — AUDIT THE EXISTING FRONTEND

Before making changes:

1. Inspect the complete existing frontend and its folder structure.
2. Identify all existing pages, components, routes, forms and navigation.
3. Understand the existing design system, colours, typography and responsiveness.
4. Identify missing pages, weak UX, confusing navigation, unnecessary steps and conversion problems.
5. Check whether the current frontend properly supports different business categories.
6. Check whether registration and onboarding are actually connected to the intended application flow.
7. Identify duplicate components and inconsistent layouts.

Create a concise audit of:

* What already exists and works.
* What is missing.
* What should be improved.
* What should be removed or simplified.

Then implement the improvements.

IMPORTANT:

* Do not unnecessarily rebuild working functionality.
* Do not break existing backend integrations, API contracts or authentication.
* Do not replace real functionality with fake buttons or dummy forms.
* Reuse existing components where appropriate.
* If backend functionality is missing, clearly identify it rather than pretending it works.

⸻

PHASE 2 — DEFINE THE QFAST BRAND AND POSITIONING

Qfast is a customer-flow management SaaS platform.

It helps businesses manage:

* Walk-in queues.
* Appointments and bookings.
* Customer check-ins.
* Service workflows.
* Queue notifications.
* Customer waiting experiences.

Qfast is designed for multiple industries:

1. Restaurants and cafés.
2. Clinics and hospitals.
3. Salons and spas.
4. Diagnostic centres.
5. Service centres.
6. Other appointment-based and queue-based businesses.

Position Qfast as a platform that helps businesses reduce unnecessary waiting, organise operations and improve customer experiences.

Possible brand headline:

“Less Waiting. Better Business.”

Supporting message:

“Manage queues, appointments and customer flow — all from one simple platform.”

The messaging must be clear, memorable and business-oriented.

Avoid positioning Qfast as a restaurant-only product.

PHASE 5 — DESIGN THE CENTRAL BUSINESS REGISTRATION FUNNEL

This is one of the most important parts of the entire platform.

All businesses must enter through one common registration experience.

Do not build separate authentication systems for restaurants, clinics and salons.

Step 1: Entry point

When someone clicks Get Started Free:

Display a clean registration page.

Offer:

* Continue with Google.
* Continue with email, if supported.
* Login for existing users.

Clearly explain that registration is free during the launch offer.

Step 2: Select business category

Ask:

“What type of business do you manage?”

Display selectable cards:

* Restaurant / Café.
* Clinic / Hospital.
* Salon / Spa.
* Diagnostic Centre.
* Service Centre.
* Other.

The selected category should determine the rest of the onboarding journey.

Step 3: Business information

Collect only essential information:

* Business name.
* Owner or manager name.
* Business email.
* Mobile number.
* Business category.
* Address.
* City.
* State.
* Country.

Use mobile verification when required.

Clearly explain why information is being requested.

Step 4: Business configuration

Show an industry-specific form.

For restaurants:

* Restaurant name.
* Restaurant type.
* Number of branches.
* Operating hours.
* Available services.
* Queue configuration.
* Optional table-management requirements.

For clinics:

* Clinic name.
* Number of doctors.
* Available departments or services.
* Operating hours.
* Appointment configuration.
* Walk-in queue configuration.

For salons:

* Salon name.
* Available services.
* Staff members.
* Service durations.
* Working hours.
* Appointment and walk-in preferences.

For diagnostics:

* Centre name.
* Available tests or services.
* Collection operating hours.
* Appointment configuration.

Do not ask every industry for the same information.

Step 5: Review and confirm

Show a summary of the business information.

Allow users to go back and edit it.

CTA: Create My Business.

Step 6: First-time onboarding success

Display a welcoming success page:

“Your business workspace is ready!”

Show the next three actions:

1. Configure your services.
2. Set up your queue.
3. Generate your business QR code.

Provide a progress indicator so owners understand what remains.

After registration, direct every business to its own dashboard configuration.

The shared dashboard structure should remain consistent, but its modules must adapt to the business category.

“The restaurant has generated its first QR code and is ready to accept customers.”

Clinic onboarding

Guide the owner through:

1. Add clinic details.
2. Add doctors or service providers.
3. Configure doctor-wise queues.
4. Set appointment availability.
5. Configure walk-in management.
6. Set notification preferences.
7. Generate QR code.
8. Preview the patient experience.

Salon onboarding

Guide the owner through:

1. Add salon details.
2. Add services.
3. Add staff or stylists.
4. Configure service durations.
5. Configure appointments and walk-ins.
6. Generate QR code.
7. Activate the system.

Make the onboarding experience progressive, simple and easy to complete.

PHASE 8 — UX AND CONVERSION PRINCIPLES

Apply these principles throughout the experience:

1. Five-second clarity: visitors should understand Qfast immediately.
2. One primary action per important screen.
3. Progressive disclosure: avoid overwhelming forms.
4. Industry-specific personalisation.
5. Consistent design between marketing, registration and dashboard.
6. Mobile-first responsive layouts.
7. Clear validation and helpful error messages.
8. Save onboarding progress when technically supported.
9. Visible progress indicators.
10. Accessible typography, colour contrast and navigation.
11. Fast page loading and optimised assets.
12. No unnecessary popups or aggressive sales tactics.
13. No fake scarcity or fabricated social proof.
14. Make pricing and product limitations transparent.

PHASE 9 — VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM

Create a cohesive, premium SaaS visual identity.

Design requirements:

* Modern, clean and professional.
* Strong typography hierarchy.
* Consistent spacing.
* Attractive but restrained colour palette.
* Excellent visual balance.
* Subtle animations and transitions.
* High-quality dashboard mockups.
* Consistent buttons and form elements.
* Clean icons.
* Beautiful responsive cards.
* Proper empty states and loading states.

Avoid:

* Excessive gradients.
* Excessive glassmorphism.
* Generic AI-generated illustrations everywhere.
* Overloaded sections.
* Too many competing CTA buttons.
* Unnecessary animations.
* Huge text blocks.
* Template-like SaaS designs.

The website must feel like a trustworthy, scalable technology company.

⸻

PHASE 10 — TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION AND QUALITY CHECKS

Implement the redesign using the existing project’s technology stack.

Requirements:

* Preserve existing authentication and API integrations.
* Maintain reusable components.
* Use shared layouts for industry pages.
* Keep industry-specific onboarding configurations modular.
* Implement proper routing.
* Ensure forms have validation.
* Ensure loading, success and error states work.
* Make all pages responsive.
* Test desktop, tablet and mobile layouts.
* Ensure navigation and CTAs lead to the correct destinations.
* Avoid broken links and placeholder buttons.
* Avoid hardcoded credentials or sensitive information.
* Maintain accessibility and reasonable performance.

Do not introduce unnecessary libraries or rewrite the backend without a clear requirement.

Final deliverables

After implementation, provide:

1. Summary of the existing frontend audit.
2. List of missing features discovered.
3. List of improvements implemented.
4. All new and updated routes.
5. Updated component structure.
6. Registration and onboarding flow.
7. Funnel tracking events implemented.
8. Features that still require backend work.
9. Any unresolved issues.

FINAL OBJECTIVE

When a restaurant owner visits Qfast, they should immediately understand why Qfast is useful for their restaurant.

When a clinic owner visits Qfast, they should see a solution designed for clinics.

The same should apply to every supported industry.

A visitor should be able to discover Qfast, understand the value, trust the platform, register, configure their business and generate their first QR code without needing personal assistance.

Build the complete experience as one coherent SaaS product.

Prioritise clarity, trust, usability, conversion and successful business activation over decorative design.
